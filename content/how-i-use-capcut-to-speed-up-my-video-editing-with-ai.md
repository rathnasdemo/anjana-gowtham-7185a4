> **TL;DR:** After four years of editing the old-school way, I've been using CapCut for a few weeks and its built-in AI has been a game changer. I walk through my usual workflow and then redo it in CapCut: two-click color correction and color match, editing bad takes and pauses by deleting text from the transcript, voice isolation and loudness normalization for audio, text behind me, auto reframe, auto captions, and long video to shorts. It sped my workflow up by over 80%.

I've been doing YouTube videos for 4 years now, and my editing has always been the old-school way: record a script, edit the A-roll manually, remove all the filler, color grade my video, fix my audio, add text and video effects, and then post it on YouTube. But with recent AI improvements, video editing shouldn't be this complex anymore, right?

I've been trying out CapCut for the past few weeks and it has been a game changer, with AI features built right into the editor: auto background removal, auto framing for Instagram, auto captions using AI, and so many more. It has made editing so much easier and honestly much faster too. I'll show you my usual workflow, and then how I use CapCut and its AI features to speed up the same workflow.

## A quick intro to CapCut

*[Watch from 0:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e5iYnMo-r0Y&t=40s)*

For those who are not aware of it, CapCut is one of the best editing tools in the market today. There's an editing app for Mac, Windows, mobile and even the web. Unlike traditional video editors, CapCut is designed to make video editing very simple and intuitive, while still bringing all the advanced features of other nonlinear editors. It has a ton of effects, transitions and AI tools built in, and the best part is that it has a free version with an awesome feature set.

## My usual editing workflow

*[Watch from 1:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e5iYnMo-r0Y&t=81s)*

This is how I usually edit my A-roll footage. For an 8 minute video I usually have around 30 to 40 minutes of raw footage, since I have some bad takes and mislines. My steps look like this:

1. **Cut the mistakes.** I go through those 40 minutes of footage and manually cut the pieces where I fumbled, misspoke or mispronounced something. Then I remove all the filler words that came in, like ums and pauses. This alone takes quite a bit of time.
2. **Color.** I usually record in log profile, where the colors are not accurate and the footage is overexposed, so I need to fix the colors and color grade it to make the video look much more pleasing.
3. **Effects.** I add text overlays, transitions, video effects, blurring out, infographics and everything.
4. **Audio.** I correct the volume, remove any background noise and level my audio properly.

As you can see, it's a lot of complex work, and it's not even the creative part. That's just for the long form. I also post reels and shorts based on my long-form videos, so there is a whole separate process where I break the long-form video down into smaller clips, frame them in the center, add captions, and do a ton more work just for social media.

These are all routine tasks, so why can't it be simpler? CapCut has made exactly all of these steps way simpler.

## Color: two clicks, and color match

*[Watch from 3:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e5iYnMo-r0Y&t=181s)*

I'll show you the exact same workflow in CapCut, where AI and the simplified tools let me do it so much quicker and better. First I open a new project on my Mac and import my raw footage to the timeline. Since I shot this video in log format, it's all washed out, dull and overexposed, so let me fix that first.

I used to correct this manually, or use a LUT, which I'm not really a fan of because it doesn't give me my favorite look. With CapCut, fixing the footage is just two clicks away. I select the clip, go to the right side panel, and under adjustments I select auto adjust and color correction. The video and colors are auto adjusted for the most part, which gives me a good baseline to begin with. Because I shot it overexposed on purpose to preserve the shadows, the only extra correction I do is to bring down the exposure and increase the contrast a little. Within seconds the clip is already looking great.

But here's the kicker. If I like a certain look from a certain clip, I can ask AI in CapCut to replicate that clip's color grading directly onto my clip, without knowing how to color grade at all. I found a clip online with cinematic colors I like, and I don't know how I could manually achieve the same look. Under the same adjustment tab there is an option called color match. I check it, CapCut asks me to select a video or photo to replicate the grade from, I drag and drop the picture I liked, and instantly CapCut has replicated the color grading onto my footage. I can adjust the intensity as I like. In another editor I would have had to be a pro and spend hours to get this right.

## Bad takes and filler: edit by deleting text

*[Watch from 5:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e5iYnMo-r0Y&t=301s)*

Color grading is done, but I haven't edited my 40-minute footage yet. I need to remove all the bad takes and filler words, and with CapCut this is like magic. I select my footage and click the transcript symbol on top of the timeline. CapCut automatically transcribes my whole script. When I play, it highlights the parts of the script as they come up, and it also shows the pauses and gaps in my video. Here you can see a bad take, where I repeated the same sentence.

To cut out the bad take, I just select the paragraph in the script and hit remove. That's it, CapCut removes that bad take from my footage automatically. I can go through, select the sentences with bad takes, hit remove, and done. The same goes for the pauses: I select that part, hit remove, and the pauses are gone.

## Fixing a misspoken word without a reshoot

*[Watch from 6:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e5iYnMo-r0Y&t=360s)*

One more piece of AI magic. In this part I misspoke the words "angled down" as "angled up". I'm noticing this only while editing, and I can't reshoot the whole section. So I select that word, fix the word I misspoke, and click the button to apply it. CapCut fixes the mistake for me in real time, without me having to reshoot the same topic. This just saved me hours of work.

## Audio: voice isolation, loudness and enhance voice

*[Watch from 6:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e5iYnMo-r0Y&t=400s)*

Color grading is done and editing is done, so let's fix the audio. If I select the clip and go to the audio tab in the right panel, I can of course change the volume, add a fade effect and so on, but there are two features here that are my favorites.

- **Voice isolation.** I played a clip with a ton of background noise, which in any other editor would be a nightmare to clean up. In CapCut I select voice isolation with AI, and it removes all the background noise and keeps just my voice.
- **Normalize loudness.** My voice may be loud in some parts and low in others. With this AI feature enabled, CapCut lowers the volume where it's loud and increases it where it's soft.

I can even enhance my voice with just one click using the enhance voice option. This again would consume a lot of time in other editors.

## Text, transitions and text behind me

*[Watch from 7:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e5iYnMo-r0Y&t=462s)*

My video is taking shape really well, and I've spent hardly 15 minutes so far. Now it's time to add text, transitions and other effects. CapCut is known for its ton of text effects, transitions and video effects, and the left panel is where I find all of them. I drag and drop a text effect onto my timeline, add a few, and add some transitions to my clips.

There's one more feature I use very often: the effect of text appearing behind me on the screen. To do it:

1. Duplicate a clip by holding down the Alt or Option key and dragging it up, so there are two copies.
2. Drop a text effect onto the timeline, change it to say whatever I want, and drag it in between the two clips.
3. To make the text visible behind me, remove the background from the top layer: select the top clip, go to the video tab, choose remove background, and select auto removal.

With AI my background has been removed for the whole clip, and I can add more effects onto the text for an even fancier video.

At this point my long-form video is pretty much complete. I can add B-roll if I want, but only my creative part is remaining. All my monotonous and mundane work has been taken care of by AI in less than 20 minutes in CapCut. Such a time saver. I can export this right away.

## Short-form videos: reframe, captions, and long video to shorts

*[Watch from 9:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e5iYnMo-r0Y&t=582s)*

For short-form videos there are usually three things I do: break my long-form video down into smaller clips, convert the landscape video into a portrait video for mobile screens while reframing my face to keep it in frame, and caption them, which is absolutely essential for reels and shorts. CapCut can help me do all three with AI.

- **Auto reframe.** I take a horizontal video and change the video ratio to 9 by 16, then expand it to fit the screen. That's fine if I did not move much, but if I was swinging or moving in the frame, I go to the video tab, and under the basic section there's an option called auto reframe. This AI option analyzes the frame and keeps me in the center for the whole clip, so I don't have to manually reframe.
- **Auto captions.** I used to create text effects for each sentence or word manually and highlight them as needed. Now, in the captions tab on the left panel, there is a section called auto captions. I select the auto highlighting keywords option and hit generate, and CapCut generates the whole caption for me. If I don't like the default look, I can pick a template I like, generate captions again, and I have captions for my whole clip in seconds, with amazing styles and effects. It does make mistakes here and there, which I can fix quickly.
- **Long video to shorts.** If you give CapCut a long-form video, it can automatically create multiple short-form clips for you with AI. On the CapCut homepage there's an option called long video to shorts. I drop in my previously edited long-form video, CapCut uploads it to its server for quick processing, then analyzes the video and creates multiple short-form clips complete with subtitles and effects, based on my requirements.

Without CapCut, creating eight shorts on my own with editing, captioning and reframing would easily have taken me hours. Now it takes just a few seconds for eight videos. In my experience it usually does an amazing job of picking the interesting and complete parts from a long-form video, and if it's not perfect you can easily edit it and customize it to your liking.
