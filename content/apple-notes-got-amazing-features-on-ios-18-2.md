*Adapted from the video [Apple Notes Got AMAZING Features on iOS 18.2!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2a1q12PIRxo)*

> **TL;DR:** Apple Notes is the best free note-taking app for Apple users, and iOS 18.2 makes it significantly better. Without Apple Intelligence, you get live audio recording with transcription, native call recording stored as searchable notes, text formatting, collapsible sections and better Math Notes. With Apple Intelligence, on iPhone 15 Pro, iPhone 16 and iPhone 16 Pro only, you get refined handwriting, image generation from text or a sketch, and writing tools including ChatGPT.

Apple Notes is the best free note-taking app for Apple users today, and with iOS 18.2 it is getting significantly better, with a ton of new features that I'm loving so far. Here I'll go over all of them. Some use Apple Intelligence, and I'll show you how I use them to improve my productivity.

## Live audio recording in Notes

*[Watch from 0:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2a1q12PIRxo&t=21s)*

The first feature, which I have started to use pretty extensively, is live audio recording directly in the Notes app. You might have seen the Voice Memos app for recording audio, but now you can do all that directly in Apple Notes. Tap the attach symbol and select record audio, and you can start recording audio memos and anything else you want to save to your notes.

The best part is that you can even live-transcribe the audio as you're speaking, and it works flawlessly. Once you're done, the recording is automatically attached to the note along with the full transcript. Since the audio is transcribed automatically, you can search with text in your notes like you normally would, and your audio notes will show up in the search results.

I used to use Google Docs to create scripts for my YouTube videos, and it takes a lot of time to type out the whole script. Now I record as I speak and the Notes app transcribes the whole thing live. It has saved me so much time, and it replaces some paid transcription apps with this free one. This feature does not require Apple Intelligence, so don't worry if you're not on the latest iPhone model.

## Call recording

*[Watch from 1:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2a1q12PIRxo&t=101s)*

Next, also related to audio recording: you can now record your phone calls natively on iPhones. We used to use third-party apps for this, but finally it's coming natively. How is it an Apple Notes feature? Because the call recordings are stored automatically in Apple Notes, with transcriptions.

When you're on a call, you should see a new call recording icon at the top left. Tap it and you'll see the option to record the call. When you start recording, there is an audio message to let the participant know that the call is being recorded. Once you end the call, the recording is stored as a new note in Apple Notes, and again the best part is that it's automatically transcribed, so it's searchable too.

## Text formatting

*[Watch from 2:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2a1q12PIRxo&t=140s)*

One of the best features of other note-taking apps has been the ability to format your notes and text in a much nicer way, and with iOS 18.2 Apple Notes is finally getting text formatting options. When you select a text or a sentence, you can highlight it and change the color in the formatting section. With this addition you can change size, formatting, color, indentation and callouts.

Let me be honest: this is still not the kind of text customization that some note-taking apps like Notion and OneNote give you, but it's still getting there.

## Collapsible sections

*[Watch from 3:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2a1q12PIRxo&t=182s)*

One more neat organization feature is collapsible sections. If you have different sections in your notes, you can collapse them for easy organization and viewing, and it's all automated as long as you have already differentiated the text using headings and subheadings.

As soon as I format a subtitle as a subheading, I can immediately see a small arrow before it. This means it's a collapsible subsection, and when I tap on it the whole section collapses neatly. My notes have gotten much cleaner after this feature.

## Math Notes

*[Watch from 3:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2a1q12PIRxo&t=222s)*

Here is one more feature that doesn't require Apple Intelligence, though you may think it does: Math Notes. It was pretty finicky before, but with iOS 18.2 it works really well.

I create a new note, select the markup icon, and use the pen to write some basic math questions. As soon as I complete one, Apple Notes automatically suggests solving it, and when I tap on it, it computes and writes down the answer so neatly that it even writes in handwriting similar to mine. I know this is basic math, but I can do it the old-fashioned way as well: I write numbers one below the other, draw a line below them, and Apple Notes solves it automatically.

I tried unit conversions like miles to kilometers, and even complex math with square roots, exponentials and graphs, and it solved them pretty quickly. I can see this being of incredible value for students.

## What Apple Intelligence adds

*[Watch from 4:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2a1q12PIRxo&t=280s)*

Now let's see what Apple Intelligence is bringing to Apple Notes. Keep in mind that these Apple Intelligence-based features are coming only to iPhone 15 Pro, iPhone 16 and iPhone 16 Pro models.

## Making my handwriting pretty

*[Watch from 5:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2a1q12PIRxo&t=302s)*

The first AI-based feature is making my handwriting pretty. I use my iPhone as well as my iPad to take notes, and I know a lot of students use Apple Notes extensively with Apple Pencil. In iOS 18 you can easily make your handwritten notes much cleaner and more legible.

Say I handwrite a sentence and it's not the best looking and it's all over the place. I long press to select the sentence, and I get an option to refine the text. Tapping it automatically makes my handwritten note much cleaner and more refined. I can long press the sentence again and this time tap straighten, and now it's all in a straight line, pretty and legible. This is a huge saving grace for handwritten note-takers.

## Image generation in Notes

*[Watch from 5:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2a1q12PIRxo&t=340s)*

Next is image generation in Notes, which again uses Apple Intelligence extensively. There are two features: generating an image from your text, and generating an image from your own rough sketch.

- **From text.** I have a section of text describing something. I select the whole text and tap add to Image Playground, and Apple Intelligence automatically generates an image based on the description. I was describing the Eiffel Tower but did not specify "Eiffel Tower" anywhere, and Apple Intelligence understood and gave me an accurate image. I'm actually impressed. This is also fully processed on device, so it's quicker and safer.
- **From a sketch.** I draw a rough sketch using the markup tool, and there is another option in the markup section called the image wand. I use it and circle over my rough sketch, and Apple Intelligence understands the sketch and creates a wonderful drawing straight into its place. I tried various different sketches and for the most part Apple Intelligence got it right.

This will again be a valuable feature for students and note-takers.

## Writing tools

*[Watch from 7:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2a1q12PIRxo&t=422s)*

Next, writing tools. I think this is the best use of Apple Intelligence in a note-taking app. In the Notes app you should see the Apple Intelligence symbol on the toolbar, and when you tap it you will see the writing tools section. There are three subsections:

1. **Free-form changes.** You select the text and describe to Apple Intelligence any changes you want. I can ask it to summarize, elaborate, change the tone, write in a different perspective, and so on.
2. **Predefined changes.** Do you want to proofread, or just get the key notes? Do you want to change the note into a table or a bulleted list? There are predefined options here, and tapping one rewrites your selected text.
3. **ChatGPT.** If these options are not good enough, or you want to write something from scratch, then comes the big guy: ChatGPT. When you tap compose, you are asking ChatGPT to generate notes for you based on your description. You can share your pre-selected note for reference and then describe your change, or simply share the whole note. There's even an option to attach a file or an image for ChatGPT's reference. Again, it works flawlessly.

The writing tools are one of my most used features as well, with the new iOS 18 Apple Intelligence and ChatGPT integration.
